02  How it runs

Four steps, no surprises.

You describe it

A message with what you need and roughly when. No form, no sales call — just tell me the problem in your own words.

A plan comes back

Scope, time and cost in writing before anything is built. If the work is not a good fit, you are told that instead of being sold to.

Built in visible steps

You see it as it grows, in pieces small enough to review. Not five changes at once that are impossible to check.

Handed over properly

Plain files you own, on hosting you control, with the passwords and the source. When the project ends, the work still runs.

04  Questions

Answered before you ask.

What does a project cost?

It depends on scope, so a number without a conversation would be a guess. Send what you have in mind and you get a written figure before any work starts.

How long does it take?

A small site is usually days rather than weeks. An app depends entirely on what it has to do. Whatever the answer is, it goes in the plan before the work begins.

Do I own the work?

Yes. Source files, design files and accounts are handed to you at the end. Nothing is held back as leverage.

Are there testimonials?

Not yet. Getwiy is opening to client work now, and inventing reviews would be the wrong way to start. Aqraez is the evidence in the meantime — a finished product you can examine screen by screen.

Who actually does the work?

One person, and the person you message is the person building it. Nothing is passed to a subcontractor without telling you.

Tell us what you are building.

A short message is enough — what it is, who it is for, and roughly when you need it. You get a written plan back, or an honest no.
